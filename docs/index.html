Six Headline Findings & Geometric Diagnosis

Why Packing Fraction (ρ) Does Not Govern Quantization Error (G)

PR #6 cited sphere-packing fractions (ρ = 0.2233 for F3 and φ = 0.746865 for CaB6) as evidence of quantization quality. Sphere packing governs channel noise resistance, whereas fine-cell source coding distortion obeys D ≈ G · V2/n, where G is the dimensionless normalized second moment of the Voronoi cell. The cube achieves G = 1/12 = 0.083333 and E8 achieves 0.071682; pyramidal Voronoi cells in Fn and CaBn have higher second moments, losing 0.68 to 1.69 dB before data distribution effects even enter.

Normalized Second Moment G: Closed-Form vs. Measured (bounds.out §7)

Precision Check: All 14 measured G values sit within 1.7 standard errors of the new closed-form expressions (0.1 and 0.9 SE on 40M-point checks for n = 8, 16). Even placing t at the pyramid centroid rather than the incentre still leaves F8 1.48 dB worse than a cube.

The 6 Empirical Conclusions in Short

1. Proposed F_n & CaB_n lose everywhere: Behind scalar mix by 0.4–1.3 dB (Gaussian) and 0.0–0.8 dB (Student-t), and behind E8 by 0.7–3.5 dB across every rate tested.
2. The cell shape is the culprit: Pyramidal Voronoi cells lose 1.69 dB (F8), 1.21 dB (F16), 1.04 dB (CaB7), and 0.68 dB (CaB15) relative to a cube.
3. Zero-search decoding holds for any 0 < t < 1: Rounding to 2m and picking the largest coordinate remainder |xk − 2mk| is exact for all t ∈ (0, 1)—the incentre plays no special role in it.
4. Free t ∈ [3.2, 4.0] creates an outlier-escape code: Five to seven times the incentre (0.586), large t abandons pyramid cells and lets one coordinate per block jump 1.7 cells past the grid edge, beating scalar in 34/40 synthetic cells.
5. Vortex codebooks match scalar, trail E8: V16 (−9.34 dB) and V64 (−14.56 dB) match optimal scalar quantisers (−9.30, −14.62 dB) but lag E8 (−10.12, −15.55 dB).
6. L880 loses to random points; 2-bit target is closed: L880 trails 880 random Gaussian points by 0.8 dB (0.4 dB whitened); at 0.70 bits/wt, Shannon's floor is −4.21 dB, ruling out matching 2-bit codes (~−9.3 dB) on weight error.

Proof Summary: Why Pyramidal Voronoi Decoding Holds for Any t ∈ (0, 1)

Step 1 · Cell Reduction

Translate x into [-1, 1]ⁿ with x1 ≥ |xj| for all j. Any point of Fn has the form p = 2m + s·t·ek with 0 < t < 1.

Step 2 · Coordinate Inequality

For l ≠ k, (xl − 2ml)² ≥ xl² since |xl| ≤ 1. On coordinate k, the nearest of 2m ± t is sign(xk)·t because midpoints sit at 0 and ±1.

Step 3 · Exact Verification

|x − p|² ≥ |x|² − 2t|xk| + t² ≥ |x − t e1|². Checked against exhaustive search on 200,000 points for n = 2..5 and 75 full codebooks (40 main + 35 sweep): max difference = 0.

Geometric Mechanism Behind larger_t.py

What t = 3.4 Actually Does: A Block-Wise Outlier Escape (Not an Incentre Lattice)

For an unbounded grid, t and 2 − t generate the exact same interior points, so nothing new appears inside the grid. At the boundary of a finite K-cell block, however, setting t ≈ 3.4 leaves K evenly spaced scalar levels on every weight while allowing one coordinate per block to jump 1.7 cells past the grid edge—paid for with log2(2n)/n bits per weight. Track 3 Empirical Results · 2-Bit & 3-Bit Planar VQ

Vortex Codebooks (V16, V64) vs. Lloyd-Fitted, Scalar, and E8

From 200 random starts for 16 vortices, gradient descent found 3 distinct stable equilibria (4 non-convergent); the lowest energy (−87.325902, reached 150 times) places 5 points on an inner ring and 11 on an outer ring, satisfying the balance equation to 5.0e-12. For 64 vortices, 100 starts yielded 7 distinct equilibria (26 non-convergent). Across all datasets, V16 and V64 perform almost identically to evenly spaced scalar levels, trail Lloyd-fitted 2D codebooks (LBG16, LBG64) by up to 3.43 dB on Student-t, and trail E8 by 0.78–1.70 dB. Notably, the lowest-energy equilibrium is not even the best codebook (on G/0, the three V16 equilibria span −9.48 to −9.27 dB).

Extension 1 · l880.out (0.6987 Bits/Weight)

The 880 Realisations (L880 & L880w) vs. Random Points & Shannon's Floor

Every stored point in rigid-realizations satisfies all 17 bar lengths (each between 1.0003 and 1.0152) to 4.4e-15. However, as a 14D codebook at log2(880)/14 = 0.6987 bits/weight, L880 is strongly anisotropic—its variance along its widest principal axis is 19.8× that along its narrowest. Even after linear whitening (L880w), it lags 880 random Gaussian points by 0.44–0.61 dB. Moreover, Shannon's rate-distortion floor at 0.6987 bits on Gaussian weights is −4.21 dB (whereas 2-bit scalar is −9.30 dB), and chaining three blocks into 880³ codewords for 42 weights is a product code with the exact same error per weight—closing PR #6's 2-bit milestone for weight error.

Key Takeaways from l880.out

19.8× Aspect Ratio: Rigid unit-length bar constraints force vertices into an elongated 14D ellipsoid, costing 0.37 dB on Gaussian data before whitening (−2.42 vs. −2.79 dB).
Worse Than Unstructured Random: 880 i.i.d. Gaussian vectors hit −3.24 dB (G), −2.98 dB (T), and −2.93 dB (Real)—beating L880w by 0.44, 0.61, and 0.57 dB.
Product-Code Invariance: Chaining 3 blocks of 14D into 42D (880³ states) preserves 0.6987 bits/weight and leaves per-weight distortion unchanged at −2.79 dB, well above the −4.21 dB Shannon limit.

Methodological Scope & Follow-Up Priorities

Six Experimental Limits & Next-Step Verification Agenda

While these results decisively resolve the weight-error claims of PR #6 under single-matrix scaling, six structural limits govern how the t ∈ [3.2, 4.0] outlier-escape lead should be tested before drawing model-level conclusions.

1. Weight Error ≠ Model Output

The second experimental record established that matrix weight MSE does not reliably predict downstream LLM perplexity or logit KL divergence.

2. Per-Matrix vs. Per-Group Scale

This run used one least-squares scale per matrix. Under per-group largest-entry scaling (e8-gain-largest-entry-rule-2026-11-17), F8 at t = 0.586 and 0.889 loses to scalar and E8 on every matrix. Large t (≈ 3.4) has not yet been tested under per-group AbsMax scaling.

3. Deliberately Weak Reference Mixes

The reference mixes pair two adjacent uniform level counts, and the E8 arm uses a basic out-of-range rule costing 0.19–0.26 dB at 2 bits. Beating a mix by 0.2 dB does not beat optimal non-uniform scalar or multi-shell E8 codes.

4. Real Matrix Slice Geometry

The 32 real matrices come from one small speech model, taking the first 131,040 weights reshaped into rows of 1,680 that do not align with original matrix rows.

5. Synthetic Sample Size

Each synthetic family (Gaussian G and Student-t4 T) was evaluated across three random seeds of 128×1,680 matrices (215,040 weights per seed).

6. Unplanned Sweep & Saddle Equilibria

larger_t.py was widened twice after inspecting results (hitting 4.0 in 30/376 runs), making it a preregistration-pending lead; vortex search only reached downhill local minima.
