# Siliquant: 8D E8 Lattice Weight Quantization Brief
## Executive Summary & Roofline Projections

## Core Hypothesis & Motivation
Large Language Model (LLM) decoding on TPU v5e at low batch sizes is strictly memory-bound, where High Bandwidth Memory (HBM) bandwidth dictates token generation speed. Siliquant addresses this bottleneck by quantizing model weights using 8-dimensional $E_8$ lattice codes (via both an integer ball $hZBG128$ arm and a Hadamard-turned $E_8$ arm) at ~4.5 bits per weight (bpw). Reducing the bytes transferred per token increases effective chip bandwidth, raising pod density and decode throughput.
## Theoretical Bandwidth Ceilings (TPU v5e)
Assuming a TPU v5e HBM bandwidth ceiling of 819 GB/s (weight-only, batch size 1, exact rational arithmetic), reducing weight precision from 16-bit (FP16) or 8-bit (INT8/FP8) to ~4.5 bpw yields the following theoretical decode throughput ceilings:

| Precision / Quantization Scheme | Bits / Weight | Qwen Decode Ceiling | Gemma Decode Ceiling | Speedup vs. FP16 | Speedup vs. INT8 |
| FP16 Baseline | 16.00 | 265.3 tok/s | 102.4 tok/s | 1.00x | — |
| INT8 / FP8 Baseline | 8.00 | — | — | 2.00x | 1.00x |
| Ball Arm ($ZBG128$) | 4.50 | 943.2 tok/s | 364.0 tok/s | 3.56x | 1.78x |
| Hadamard-Turned $E_8$ | 4.47 | 949.5 tok/s | 366.4 tok/s | 3.58x | 1.79x |

Note: Ratios derive directly from bit-width compression ($16 / 4.50 = 3.56\times$; $8 / 4.47 = 1.79\times$) and represent theoretical memory-bandwidth upper bounds. Practical kernel execution speeds are detailed in Section 4.

Gains are computed from the unrounded six-decimal mean KLs (for Arm A, `site/results.html` lines 119-121: Qwen2.5 0.018875 vs 0.033348, Llama-3.2 0.036038 vs 0.043179), so a gain can differ by up to 0.2 points from one computed with the four-decimal KLs shown.


* Outlier Mitigation (Ten-Column Repair): Preserving 10 high-magnitude `ffn_down` columns in full precision (+0.005 bpw overhead) allows the plain integer ball to outperform $Q4\_K\_M$ across all three models (KL: `0.0222` Qwen, `0.0528` SmolLM2, `0.0302` Llama).
* Downstream Validation: A bit-exact packed file at 4.505 bpw maintains MMLU accuracy within -0.70 to +0.15 points of the 16-bit model while reducing total error counts below $Q4\_K\_M$ by 10.2 (Qwen), 13.5 (SmolLM2), and 11.9 (Llama).
* Data-Free Importance Matrices: Matrices constructed from random vocabulary pieces recover 77% (Qwen), 96% (SmolLM2), and 69% (Llama) of the quality achieved by real-text calibration matrices.

## 2. Scaling Dynamics & Structural Weight Probes

## Quantization Scale & Sensitivity Analysis

* Scale Selection Rule: Per-row maximum scaling preserves the top-1 token on 90% of evaluated positions, substantially outperforming per-matrix least-squares scaling (62% top-1 retention).
* Clipping vs. Rounding Sensitivity: Clipping just 0.17% of extreme weights degrades SNR to -27.5 dB (KL: `0.318` ), whereas uniform 5-bit rounding yields -21.7 dB (KL: `0.118` ), confirming high sensitivity to weight outliers.
* Error Prediction: Spectral rank correlation serves as a strong predictor of quantization degradation ($r = 0.889$, squared error $0.603$).

## Exact Structural Closures (Weight Sparsity)

* Context-Specific Zero-Load Weights: Up to 2.79B weights in Gemma can be zeroed out for a specific text sequence with 0.000000 KL divergence.
* Universal Core Sparsity: A subset of 55M weights can be zeroed across any tested text with 0.000000 KL divergence, indicating a structural dead-weight core.

## 3. Systems Implementation & Current Bottlenecks

## Decoder Architecture & Verification

* Algebraic Construction: $E_8$ is constructed via an index-4 glue group over two copies of $D_4$ (the union of four cosets).
* Branchless Integer Decoder: Implemented with zero dynamic branches using mandatory `int64` lanes (52-bit intermediate peaks). Passes 48/48 exact squared-distance audit vectors.

## Current Runtime Bottlenecks (Work in Progress)
While quality metrics and roofline models validate the quantization scheme, CPU decode throughput currently sits well below hardware bandwidth limits:

* Packed Codec Throughput: The fastest research codec (`E8 compact 88b` ) decodes at 58–65M weights/sec/core (up from the `ballpack` baseline of 18–23M weights/sec/core), remaining orders of magnitude below the 45,000M weights/sec target required to saturate memory bandwidth.
* Fused GEMV Overhead: On a Zen 1 CPU, the fused Hadamard $E_8$ GEMV kernel runs 178x to 220x slower than a standard $Q4$ proxy, with 72–75% of stage time spent in Stage-1 leaf unranking.

## 4. Limitations & Next Experimental Steps

## Scope & Boundary Conditions

   1. Bit-Width Operating Window: The optimal operating regime lies between 4.0 and 4.5 bpw (with KL continuing to improve up to 5.0 bpw; e.g., Gemma 3 4B WikiText KL drops from `0.131` at 4.0 bpw $\rightarrow$ `0.072` at 4.5 bpw $\rightarrow$ `0.038` at 5.0 bpw). Below the 4.0 bpw INT4 roofline floor, KL divergence degrades sharply.
   2. Evaluation Boundaries: Gemma 4 E4B benchmarks use an 8-bit reference on a single text; Gemma 3 benchmarks use a 16-bit reference across two domains. Furthermore, zero-codeword behavior is model-dependent, and the observed output law holds on two tested models but breaks on the third.
   
## Immediate Roadmap

   1. Kernel Acceleration (GPU / TPU): Port the branchless decoder and fused GEMV kernel to TPU v5e (Pallas/XLA) and GPU (CUDA/Triton) to measure real-world HBM bandwidth utilization and eliminate the CPU Stage-1 leaf-unranking bottleneck.
   2. Stage-2 Certificate Verification: Complete verification of the packed-file codec and Stage-2 certificate pipeline.
   3. Cost-Quality Pareto Frontier: Pair 4.5–5.0 bpw accuracy curves with measured TPU v5e kernel latencies to pinpoint the exact serving-cost sweet spot.
