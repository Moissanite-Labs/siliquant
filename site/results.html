Quantisation results

At about 4.5 bits per weight, turned E8 gave the lowest mean KL among three ball codebooks on Qwen2.5, SmolLM2 and Llama-3.2. On Gemma 3 270M and 1B, the calibrated integer ball beat calibrated Q4_K_M on both texts at about 5 bpw, with mixed results near 4.5 bpw; on 4B it won on both texts at 4.5040 bpw versus 4.8151 bpw, an unequal-rate comparison. The separate Gemma 4 E4B bridge matches the earlier Q8_0-based result within the preregistered tolerance.

Siliquant rounds a group of weights jointly to a point in a finite codebook, cut from the integer grid or the eight-dimensional E8 lattice. The cut and the lattice orientation change which vectors can be represented at a given bit budget. The studies below ask what survives when the rounded weights are put back into the model: mean KL divergence measures the departure from the reference model’s next-token distribution (lower is closer). Bits per weight (bpw) describe the quantised matrices, not whole-model size.

Open PR #41 · measured

Turning E8 pays at 4.5 bpw, not uniformly at 3

One ball per 128 weights, no calibration or kept columns. Turned E8 and plain E8 have nearly identical aggregate weight error, yet their output KL differs. At 4.5 bpw the Qwen2.5 gap between them is only 1.1 combined standard errors; it is 5.4 for SmolLM2 and 5.0 for Llama.

On main · measured

Integer balls survive the write to disk

The packed format stores 32 weights in 18 bytes before kept columns and other file data. The experiment wrote the file, read it back and scored the reconstructed model. Keeping the ten columns of each feed-forward down projection most affected by clipping reduced Llama’s KL by 34.5%, from 0.051877 to 0.033987, at 4.50542 bpw. The same repair barely moves Qwen2.5 or SmolLM2.

A second text

On a Python source sample, SmolLM2’s 128-weight integer ball with a WikiText-derived importance matrix scores 0.016664 against 0.023435 for Q4_K_M with that matrix. On Qwen2.5, the plain ball scores 0.013971, the ball with the importance matrix 0.015366, and Q4_K_M with that same matrix 0.019446. This test uses counted balls, not the 32-weight packed files above.

Source: Python and WikiText scores (main snapshot).

Downstream accuracy

On 2,000 MMLU questions, the calibrated ball versus calibrated Q4_K_M differed by −0.10 ± 0.55 percentage points on Qwen2.5 and +0.75 ± 0.51 on SmolLM2 (paired standard errors). The subset does not establish an accuracy advantage for the calibrated ball.

Source: MMLU scores and paired differences (main snapshot).

Gemma: calibration and the E4B bridge

4B: the calibrated ball beats stock at its normal rate

The 4.5040-bpw ball scores lower than calibrated Q4_K_M on both texts with the same WikiText-derived importance matrix. Q4_K_M runs at its normal 4.8151 bpw, so this is not a rate-matched comparison. The 4B stock receipts also flag a change to the token embedding (token_embd.weight), outside the counted blocks; the ball runner copies it unchanged from the reference.

270M and 1B: the calibrated ball needs a higher rate

At practically the same block rate, the uncalibrated 128-weight ball cuts mean KL by 58–63% versus Q4_0 across these two models and texts. Calibrated Q4_K_M is a stronger control: at about 4.5 bpw, the calibrated ball is lower only for 270M on WikiText. At about 5 bpw it is lower in all four comparisons, against stock rates of about 5.43–5.48 bpw.

Gemma 4 E4B: the Q8_0 bridge

The preregistered P7 check connects this sweep to the earlier E4B result on main. The two E8 Voronoi variants were scored against Q8_0, not BF16, and there is no stock comparison arm in these receipts. Both run at 4.5000 block bits per weight; the receipts record 258 of 294 block matrices as active and a reference perplexity of 7.595148.

Mean KL (± standard error) was 0.074682 ± 0.002264 for centred VE832:16 and 0.077252 ± 0.002314 for uncentred VE8Z32:16. The centred result meets the preregistered tolerance of ±0.002 around the earlier 0.0747; that earlier run also recorded 0.1173 for scalar rounding at the same block rate.

Bridge sources at 86bb1b2: scores and JSONL.

Open PRs #35 and #36 · kernel benchmarks

Smaller tables have not closed the decode gap

At the 88-bit point budget, the compact E8⁴ decoder in #35 needs 19,122 hot bytes rather than the earlier BallIndex table’s 1,376,760. That is a working-set comparison, not model storage. In #36, the 4.5-bpw fused turned-E8 matrix-vector kernel was still about 178–220× slower than a byte-matched custom Q4 proxy on one Zen 1 CPU. The comparator was not llama.cpp Q4_K_M; neither benchmark measures serving throughput.

Sources: PR #35 · working-set measurements · PR #36 · GEMV measurements.
