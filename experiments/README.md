# Experiment records

One folder per study. Each record states its question, how it was run, what came out and its limits. A record is evidence for what it measured and nothing more.

Statuses are those of [weft-loom's index](https://github.com/Moissanite-Labs/weft-loom/blob/main/experiments/README.md): `complete`, `partial`, `pilot`, `exploratory`, `design`, `withdrawn`, `superseded`.

| Record | Question | Status | Plan before data | Result in one line |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [lattice-vs-scalar-rounding-2026-11-15](lattice-vs-scalar-rounding-2026-11-15/README.md) | At equal bits per weight, does rounding blocks of 8 weights to the lattice A8\* or E8 give lower weight error than rounding each weight alone, with and without balancing rows and columns? | `complete` | yes, commit `195f06e` | E8 beats scalar rounding on every matrix. In the mean of each family A8\* gains less than half as much. The brief's claim for balanced A8\* holds on 32 real matrices (0.39 dB) and misses on the synthetic family (0.16 dB). Weight error only. |
| [model-output-gemma-e4b-2026-11-15](model-output-gemma-e4b-2026-11-15/README.md) | Does a smaller weight error give a closer output on a real language model, and what does the output follow if not? | `exploratory` | yes, commit `60ec5aa`, then extended five times as results came in | No. In these runs the output followed how the scale is set: one scale per row or per 32 weights, from the largest entry. With that rule E8 gives a mean KL of 0.075 at 4.5 bits against 0.117 for llama.cpp's `Q4_0`. Pulling back 0.17 percent of the weights does more damage than the best 5-bit rounding of all of them. Two closures score exactly as the unchanged model. 10 of 18 predictions hold. One model, one text. |
| [incentre-vortex-codebooks-2026-11-17](incentre-vortex-codebooks-2026-11-17/README.md) | Do the codebooks that PR #6 proposes (F_n and CaB_n on the incentre lattice, vortex equilibria in the plane, the 880 realisations of a graph) give a lower weight error than scalar rounding or E8 at equal bits? | `complete` | yes, commit `61c552b`, extended once and amended once before any result. One sweep was added after the results were read | As proposed all of them lose. F_n and CaB_n are behind a scalar mix by 0.0 to 1.3 dB and behind an E8 mix by 0.7 to 3.5 dB at equal bits, and their cells are worth 0.7 to 2.0 dB less than a cube. A vortex codebook is about as good as the best scalar quantiser. The 880 realisations are a worse codebook than 880 random points. With the points of F_n moved five to seven times further out, which the plan did not have, the code beats the scalar mix in 38 of 40 synthetic cells and the E8 mix in 8, all under 2 bits. 6 of 8 predictions hold. Weight error only, one scale per matrix. |
| [e8-gain-largest-entry-rule-2026-11-17](e8-gain-largest-entry-rule-2026-11-17/README.md) | Under the rule that clips nothing, E8 gained 1.5 to 2.5 dB of weight error over scalar rounding on gemma, when its cell is worth 0.65 dB. Where does the rest come from, and does F_8 of PR #6 do better? | `complete` | yes, commit `61c552b` | The gain needs no model: 1.36 dB on Gaussian data with groups of 32 and 2.3 dB per row. Per row it is 0.65 dB of cell and 1.64 dB of scale, because the region of the E8 code is nearly a ball. One block of each group lies on a wall of its cell, which costs 0.26 dB with groups of 32. F_8 loses to the scalar rule and to E8 on every matrix, with more bits. 4 of 6 predictions hold. Weight error only, and not run on gemma. |
| [region-shapes-no-clip-rule-2026-11-17](region-shapes-no-clip-rule-2026-11-17/README.md) | Under the same rule, what do the integers and E8 give when the codebook is cut by a ball or a cross-polytope and not by the lattice's own cell? | `complete` | yes, commit `6964d05` | Integer levels in a ball reach 76 to 89 percent of the E8 Voronoi code's gain on the real matrices, with no lattice. E8 cut by a ball beats the E8 Voronoi code by 0.13 to 0.20 dB on 29 to 32 of 32 real matrices. E8 cut by a cross-polytope beats it per row by 0.4 to 0.5 dB in the mean, on 18 and 20 of 32. 5 of 6 predictions hold. Weight error only, at no more bits, with no packed format. |
